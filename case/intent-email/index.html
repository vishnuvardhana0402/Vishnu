    • 02 PEOPLE AND MY SCOPE

    Help keeping up, without handing over what gets sent.

    People who run their work through email on a work Gmail or Outlook account and want help keeping up, while every message that leaves still goes out on their say-so.

    As Product Designer at Huemn Interactive, I designed how mail is labelled, how replies and follow-ups are drafted, how the assistant learns a writing style, and the settings to steer it.

    03 THE DESIGN QUESTION

    How do people direct an assistant that acts before they ask, and stay in control?

    Every decision below is a version of the same trade-off: let the assistant take work off people’s hands, but keep each step

  • Visible Every action leaves something to see: a label, a draft, a brief.
  • Reviewable Nothing goes out until the person sends it.
  • Easy to change A drag, a switch or a sentence changes what it does.

    • 04 DECISION · CATEGORIZATION

    People decide where each label goes.

  • Stays in inbox Labelled mail stays where people look first.
  • Skips inbox Mail waits under its label instead of crowding the inbox.
  • Off The label isn’t applied at all.
  • Moving a label Drag it to another lane, or open it for the same options as a menu.
  • A label in plain words “Which emails belong here?” is answered in a sentence, rather than built from rules.
  • Its lane, before it exists The dialog shows where the new label will land.
  • How it will look The tag on a sample inbox row, before it’s created.
  • Drafts, if wanted A switch to have replies prepared for this kind of mail.
  • One main switch, “Sort my email”, turns labelling on or off; when it’s off, the rest of the page dims and can’t be used.
  • “Keep labels I’ve added myself” leaves a person’s own labels untouched.
  • Some moves aren’t allowed: an “Actioned” label can’t be set to stay in the inbox.
  • A four-step choice sets how strictly marketing mail is filtered.

    • 05 DECISION · WRITING STYLE

    Describe the voice, see the reply change.

  • In their own words “How should it write?” is a sentence, not a form.
  • Tones in one tap Be concise and professional, avoid filler words, and more.
  • Rules to always follow An “Always remember” list.
  • The reply follows A sample reply rewrites itself with every change.

    • 06 DECISION · DRAFTS AND FOLLOW-UPS

    Prepared, never sent on its own.

  • Email arrives It’s read and labelled
  • Draft prepared Left in the thread
  • Review and change In their own inbox
  • Send Only they can
  • No reply yet After the days they chose
  • Follow-up drafted In the same thread
  • Send Again, only they can
  • Drafts, not sends One switch for drafting. The prototype’s own line: “nothing goes out on its own unless you allow it.”
  • Nothing piles up Unused drafts are deleted after the days chosen.
  • Follow-ups are drafts too Written in the thread, to review and send.
  • The person sets the timing How long to wait is a setting, not a guess.

    • Drafts can end with a signature per mailbox, or a default one. With nothing set, the page says so plainly: drafts end without one.
