Intent Email

How people direct proactive email assistance and stay in control of it. Intent Email labels mail, prepares replies and follow-ups, sends a morning brief, and lets people talk to it by replying to an email.

Product Designer, Huemn InteractiveWork Gmail and OutlookLive product

01Context

An assistant that works where the mail already is.

What it isIntent Email is Ve’s email assistant. It connects to a work Gmail or Outlook account and works inside that inbox: it labels incoming mail, prepares replies and follow-ups, and writes a short morning brief.

Where it livesPeople don’t switch to a new mail client. They set the assistant up and steer it from a web app, and the work it prepares shows up in their own inbox, as labels, drafts in the thread and an email they can reply to.

Who it’s forPeople who run their work through email on a work Gmail or Outlook account and want help keeping up with it, without handing over control of what gets sent.

My partAs Product Designer at Huemn Interactive, I designed Intent Email’s experience: how mail is labelled, how replies and follow-ups are drafted, how the assistant learns a writing style, and the settings people use to steer it.

About this case study

Live product, illustrative prototype. Intent Email is live. The screens on this page come from my high-fidelity prototype, with sample names and data, not from a customer account. Where a screen is a demo rather than the product, it says so.

Not the Ve Browser project. My Ve Browser case study is a different, unlaunched AI-browser concept. This one is about the email assistant.

02The design question

The design question

How do people direct an assistant that acts on their email before they ask, and still stay in control?

Every decision below is a version of the same trade-off: let the assistant take work off people’s hands, but keep each step visible, reviewable and easy to change.

03Decision · Categorization

People decide where each label goes.

The questionThe assistant labels every incoming email. How do people see, and change, what happens to each kind of mail?

What I designedEvery label sits in one of three lanes: Stays in inbox, Skips inbox or Off. The lane decides what happens to that mail, and moving a label is a drag.

  • Stays in inbox

    Labelled mail stays where people look first.

  • Skips inbox

    Mail waits under its label instead of crowding the inbox.

  • Off

    The label isn’t applied at all.

  • Moving a label

    Drag it to another lane, or open it for the same options as a menu.

A label in plain words.

A new label is described in a sentence, “Which emails belong here?”, rather than built from rules. The dialog previews which lane it lands in and how the tag looks on an email, before it’s created.

Optional detail: smaller choices on this page
  • One main switch, “Sort my email”, turns labelling on or off; when it’s off, the rest of the page dims and can’t be used.
  • “Keep labels I’ve added myself” leaves a person’s own labels untouched.
  • Some moves aren’t allowed: an “Actioned” label can’t be set to stay in the inbox.
  • A four-step choice sets how strictly marketing mail is filtered.

04Decision · Writing style

Describe the voice. See the reply change.

The questionDrafts are only useful if they sound like the person sending them. How does someone teach the assistant their voice?

What I designedThe style is set in the person’s own words, with a few one-tap tones and rules to always follow, next to a sample reply that rewrites itself as the settings change.

05Decision · Drafts and follow-ups

Prepared, never sent on its own.

The questionAn assistant that writes replies could also send them. Where should it stop?

What I designedWhen a reply is needed, the draft waits in the thread; the person reviews it, changes it and sends it. Follow-ups work the same way: if someone hasn’t replied, a follow-up is drafted in the thread.

  • Drafts, not sends

    The person sends. The prototype puts it in one line: “nothing goes out on its own unless you allow it.”

  • Nothing piles up

    Drafts that aren’t used are deleted after a chosen number of days.

  • The person sets the timing

    How long to wait before a follow-up is a setting, not a guess.

Optional detail: signatures

Drafts can end with a signature per mailbox, or a default one. With nothing set, the page says so plainly: drafts end without one.

06Morning brief and conversation

A brief in the inbox. Replies are the conversation.

Each morning the assistant sends a short brief. To ask it for something, people reply to that email, so even talking to the assistant happens in the inbox.

07System and handoff

Two surfaces, one assistant.

The split is the core of the design: settings and steering live in the web app, and everything the assistant prepares lands in the inbox.

Web appLabels, writing style, drafting and follow-up settings, signatures
→
Intent EmailReads new mail and prepares work
→
Your inboxLabels, drafts in the thread, follow-ups, the morning brief
Replying to the brief sends instructions back to the assistant

09Outcomes

What shipped, and what isn’t measured yet.

Shipped

  • Intent Email is live, on work Gmail and Outlook.
  • Labelling with three lanes and labels described in plain words.
  • Drafted replies and follow-ups that wait in the thread for the person.
  • A writing style set in the person’s own words.
  • A morning brief, and conversation by replying to it.

Not measured yet

  • Time saved.
  • How often drafts are sent as written, edited or discarded.
  • How accurate the labels are.

No results are claimed on this page. Numbers will be added only when they come from real measurement.
