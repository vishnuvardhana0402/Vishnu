01 · Context

An assistant that works where the mail already is

Intent Email is Ve's email assistant. It connects to a work Gmail or Outlook account and works inside that inbox: it labels incoming mail, prepares replies and follow-ups, and writes a short morning brief.

People don't switch to a new mail client. They set the assistant up and steer it from a web app, and the work it prepares shows up in their own inbox, as labels, drafts in the thread and an email they can reply to.

About this case study

02 · People and my scope

Who it's for, and what I designed

It's for people who run their work through email on a work Gmail or Outlook account and want help keeping up with it, without handing over control of what gets sent.

As Product Designer at Huemn Interactive, I designed Intent Email's experience: how mail is labelled, how replies and follow-ups are drafted, how the assistant learns a writing style, and the settings people use to steer it.

03 · Design question

The design question

How do people direct an assistant that acts on their email before they ask, and still stay in control?

Every decision below is a version of the same trade-off: let the assistant take work off people's hands, but keep each step visible, reviewable and easy to change.

04 · Decision · Categorization

Labels decide where mail goes, and people decide where labels go

The assistant labels every incoming email. Each label sits in one of three lanes, and the lane decides what happens to that mail.

  1. Stays in inboxLabelled mail stays where people look first.
  2. Skips inboxMail waits under its label instead of crowding the inbox.
  3. OffThe label isn't applied at all.
  4. Moving a labelDrag it to another lane, or open it for the same options as a menu.

Creating a label in plain words

A new label is described in a sentence, "Which emails belong here?", rather than built from rules. The dialog previews which lane it lands in and how the tag looks on an email, before it's created.

Optional detail: smaller choices on this page
  • One main switch, "Sort my email", turns labelling on or off; when it's off, the rest of the page dims and can't be used.
  • "Keep labels I've added myself" leaves a person's own labels untouched.
  • Some moves aren't allowed: an "Actioned" label can't be set to stay in the inbox.
  • A four-step choice sets how strictly marketing mail is filtered.

05 · Decision · Writing style

Teach the voice by describing it, and see the result right away

Drafts are only useful if they sound like the person sending them. So the writing style is set in the person's own words, a few one-tap tone choices and a list of rules to always follow, next to a sample reply that rewrites itself as the settings change.

Left out on purpose: the prototype's "intent model" score ring (it starts at 72 and adds points per setting). It's setup logic, not a measure of AI accuracy, so it is cropped out and not used as evidence.

06 · Decision · Drafts and follow-ups

Prepared, never sent on its own

When a reply is needed, the assistant writes a draft and leaves it in the thread. The person reviews it, changes it and sends it. The same boundary applies to follow-ups: if someone hasn't replied, a follow-up is drafted in the thread for the person to review and send.

  1. Drafts, not sendsThe person sends. The prototype puts it in one line: "nothing goes out on its own unless you allow it."
  2. Nothing piles upDrafts that aren't used are deleted after a chosen number of days.
  3. The person sets the timingHow long to wait before a follow-up is a setting, not a guess.
Optional detail: signatures

Drafts can end with a signature per mailbox, or a default one. With nothing set, the page says so plainly: drafts end without one.

Check: the "nothing goes out on its own unless you allow it" line is prototype copy. Confirm it matches how the live product behaves before publishing.

07 · Morning brief and email conversation

A brief in the inbox, and a conversation by reply

Each morning the assistant sends a short brief. To ask it for something, people reply to that email, so even talking to the assistant happens in the inbox.

08 · System and handoff

Two surfaces, one assistant

The split is the core of the design: settings and steering live in the web app, and everything the assistant prepares lands in the inbox.

Web appLabels, writing style, drafting and follow-up settings, signatures
→
Intent EmailReads new mail and prepares work
→
Your inboxLabels, drafts in the thread, follow-ups, the morning brief
Replying to the brief sends instructions back to the assistant

Outcomes

What shipped, and what isn't measured yet

Shipped

  • Intent Email is live, on work Gmail and Outlook.
  • Labelling with three lanes and labels described in plain words.
  • Drafted replies and follow-ups that wait in the thread for the person.
  • A writing style set in the person's own words.
  • A morning brief, and conversation by replying to it.

Not measured yet

  • Time saved.
  • How often drafts are sent as written, edited or discarded.
  • How accurate the labels are.

No results are claimed on this page. Numbers will be added only when they come from real measurement.
