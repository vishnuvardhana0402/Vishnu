Buying a phone meant a trip to the store.

Top-10 Mobiles wanted to sell online. Each side of the order needed one thing.

Easy online access.

Order from home, then get it delivered, or pick it up at a store nearby.

A simple app, few screens.

The people delivering are Top-10 Mobiles’ own store staff. They needed a minimal app: what to collect, where to take it, and proof it arrived.

Check it every time it changes hands.

At each hand-off one side shows a code and the other checks it. The order only moves on when they match.

Rich for buyers, simple for staff.

Two very different people use these apps, so each one was designed for its own.

For the people buying.

Rich enough to compare phones, choose delivery or store pickup, and follow the order to the door.

For the staff delivering.

Kept minimal: one clear next step on each screen, and a swipe for each big one.

Customer app

Shop the store near you, then follow the order until it's in your hand.

The right phone in a few taps.

THE PROBLEM Phones look alike in a long list, and the details that decide it are easy to miss on a small screen.

THE DESIGN Search that suggests as you type, filters with a count beside each option, and a product page that shows what matters before the first scroll.

The store near you, its offers, every category, and any order already on its way.

Suggestions come up as you type.

Price, rating and discount on every card, and Add right there.

Filter by brand, price, colour, RAM or rating, with a count beside each option.

When it arrives, the price, offers, EMI and the points it earns.

Customer app GETTING IT

Bring it to me, or I'll pick it up.

THE PROBLEM Some people want it at the door. Others would rather collect it, but only if a store nearby has it today.

THE DESIGN The choice sits on the product page. Pickup shows which stores near a pin code have it today, and how far they are.

Home delivery

Drop a pin, add the flat and floor, and say who it's for.

Store pickup

Enter a pin code to see which stores have it today, and how far they are.

Every step, as it happens.

THE PROBLEM Once someone has paid for a phone, they want to know exactly where it is.

THE DESIGN One screen that moves through every status, with the partner's name, a call button and the delivery code as soon as they're assigned.

The store has the order, with the time it should arrive.

Packed at the store while a delivery partner is found.

The delivery code appears, with the partner’s name and a call button.

The partner on the map, with the time left.

A few minutes away. Show the partner the code when they arrive.

Rate the delivery and see the points it earned.

Customer app OPEN BOX

Open it before you accept it.

THE PROBLEM A sealed box can hide the wrong model, or a damaged one.

THE DESIGN The partner opens the box in front of the customer. They check the phone, then accept it, or raise a problem there and then.

Customer app STORE PICKUP

Or collect it from a store nearby.

THE PROBLEM Once it’s ordered, people need to know when it’s ready, where the store is, and how to prove the order is theirs.

THE DESIGN The store’s hours and directions while it’s packed, a code to show at the counter, and the option to send someone else.

The store, its hours and directions, while the order is packed.

Someone else can collect it for you.

A pickup code to show at the counter.

It moves to Picked up in your orders.

Every order stays easy to find.

THE PROBLEM After it arrives, people come back for the invoice, a return, or help.

THE DESIGN Orders split into deliveries and pickups, each with its bill and invoice, and answers sorted by topic.

Delivery partner app

What to collect, where to go, and proof that it arrived. Everything a partner needs on a shift.

Checked before the first order.

THE PROBLEM Partners carry expensive phones to people's homes, so the store has to know who they are.

THE DESIGN Sign-up with a phone number, a store to work from, and documents the admin checks before any order is assigned.

A one-time code, no password to forget.

The store they’ll collect orders from, found by name or area.

A profile photo, Aadhaar, PAN and a driving licence, each with its own camera guide.

The admin reviews them, and a notification says when it’s done.

A clear photo, the first time.

THE PROBLEM A blurry photo of a card means a rejection, and the partner can't start.

THE DESIGN A frame for the card and a preview before it's sent. If one is rejected, the partner sees which one and why, and only redoes that one.

Delivery app ON SHIFT

One order, from the store to the door.

THE PROBLEM Partners check the app on the move, and a wrong tap can push an order to the wrong step.

THE DESIGN One clear next step on every screen, and a swipe, not a tap, for each big one: accept, picked, reached, delivered.

One switch. While it’s off, no orders are assigned.

A swipe on the order card, with the items listed above it.

The store marks it ready. The partner shows the pickup code at the counter.

The items, the customer and the address, with call and map one tap away.

The route on the map, and a swipe when they arrive.

Delivery app THE HAND-OFF

Delivered only when the code matches.

THE PROBLEM At the door, anyone could say the order is theirs.

THE DESIGN The customer reads out the code on their phone and the partner types it in. A wrong code doesn't go through.

Delivery appWhen it goes wrong

A bad day still has a next step.

The problemRain, traffic or no one home. Without a way to say so, the customer is left waiting.

The designA delay or a reschedule takes a reason and a few taps, and the customer's app shows what happened and the new time.

The bad moments are designed too.

The problemNo signal, a closed store, a phone out of stock. A blank screen leaves people stuck.

The designEach one has its own screen that says what happened and, where it can, what to do: try again, or get notified.

Buy without going in, choose delivery or pickup, and check the phone before they accept it.

Know what to collect and where to take it, with proof of every drop.

A code at the counter and at the door, and a video of every unboxing.
