The AI browser that stays one step ahead of you.

VE Browser sees what you're working on: your emails, your meetings, your tabs, your whole day. It works out what comes next and has it ready before you get there. All you do is decide.

Teams work across many tools that don’t talk to each other.

Meetings, email, chat, research and code each hold one piece of the work. Nothing connects them, so people do it by hand: copying, pasting and re-explaining the same things all day.

What founders told us

We pay for too much.
I don’t know who’s doing what.
I explain the same thing five times a day.
Nothing moves unless I chase it.
Just give me one place.

The obvious fix. One surface that holds all your tools. Anyone can build it. But you’d still be the one spotting what needs doing, and doing it. Teams need an AI that does that part for them.

How might we build an AI that sees your whole day, knows what comes next and has it ready, while you stay the one who decides?

A proactive AI that works out what you need next and prepares it before you ask. All that’s left is for you to decide.

I designed browser from first idea to final screen.

I set direction with the founder each week, put early versions in front of founders of product companies to see how they really used them, and shared flows with engineers early so the design matched what the AI could actually do.

The Dynamic Notch

We turned the empty notch at the top of a MacBook into a quiet AI assistant. It stays out of your way and speaks up only when something needs you, like a meeting about to start or a reply worth sending.

One tap, and the reply writes itself.

Tap fn once. It reads what is on your screen and types the right reply straight into the box you are typing in, whether that is a message, an email or a draft.

Say it once. It writes it, or it does it.

Hold fn and talk. Speak your thoughts freely and it hands them back as clean, polished writing. Or give it a few tasks at once, and it gets each one done while you keep working.

Never miss a meeting, or a word of it.

It reminds you before every meeting, and again five minutes before, with a Join button in the notch. Join, and it listens, takes the notes and hands you a summary with every action item.

Emails that come with the answer ready.

When an email needs you, the notch tells you who it is from and sums it up in one line, with a reply already drafted in your voice. Adjust it and send, or keep it for later.

Reply without leaving your work.

A new message appears in the notch with a reply already written. Send it in one click, with an undo if you change your mind, and stay in the app you were working in.

    Then we hit a wall

    It did the work. It just didn’t sound like you.

    The Dynamic Notch worked: it caught meetings early, kept drafts ready and wrote your replies. But its words never sounded like you, and it only moved once something had already arrived.

    Its replies were right, but they didn’t sound like you.

    It saw the screen in front of you, not the week behind it.

    It waited for something to arrive before it moved.

    So the AI Browser had two jobs

    Job 1 · Make it personal

    Your voice, only sharper.

    Every draft should read the way you write: your tone, your length, your habits. Just clearer and more polished than a rushed first try.

    Job 2 · Stay one step ahead

    Ready before you ask.

    Instead of waiting for something to happen, it should know your day, your meetings and your notes, and have the next step ready before you get there.

    From the Dynamic Notch to the AI Browser

  • Dynamic Notch: Replies that read like anyone could have sent them. AI Browser: Replies in your own voice, only more polished.
  • Dynamic Notch: Saw only the screen in front of you, never the week behind it. AI Browser: Knows your meetings, notes, mail and code.
  • Dynamic Notch: Reacted once a meeting, an email or a message arrived. AI Browser: Plans ahead, so your day is ready before you start.

    • The AI Browser

    Voice and the fn key came with us from the notch. Everything else follows one promise: Ve does the preparing, you do the deciding.

    It prepares, you decide.

    Open your laptop to a morning that is already handled. Ve reads your email, calendar, Slack and docs and prepares the next steps. You decide each one, and nothing goes out without you.

    It learns your habits, but never assumes.

    Ve notices the jobs you repeat, like the Friday report, and asks, “Want me to take this over?” A routine runs only after you turn it on, and any of them can be switched off in one place.

    Walk into every meeting prepared.

    Your meetings arrive prepared: what to know, the agenda and a Join button. After the call, the recap holds the summary, the decisions and every action item with its owner.

    The tab you need, before you look for it.

    Client call in ten minutes? Ve has already found the Meet link in your Gmail and opened the tab, so you just join. It works only from your own calendar and mail, and shows where the link came from.

    Code where the context already is.

    A full coding space inside the browser, with the same assistant beside you. It already knows the meeting notes and the decision behind the change, so you never explain the why again.

    Replies drafted, you decide what goes out.

    Your email arrives sorted by what needs you, each one with a reply drafted in your voice. Read it, change it if you like, and send. Nothing goes out on its own.

    Your whole day, prepared. On your Mac and in your pocket.

    The wall became steps.

    Ve isn’t launched yet, so there are no numbers to show. What there is: a direction that works, built one step at a time. Two steps are built, one is being built, and two are still ahead.

    What I’m taking with me

    Designing for AI is designing for trust.

    The moment the AI hands the decision back to you matters as much as the work it did.

    The smallest interface that does the job wins.

    Not everything needs a dashboard. Sometimes a notch, one tab or one drafted reply is enough.

    Talk to every role, not just founders.

    Next time, I’d sit with the designer, PM, recruiter and salesperson in one company and ask what they’d really use every day.

    What I loved most was designing how people and AI work together. The AI takes the boring work off your plate. You stay the one in charge.
